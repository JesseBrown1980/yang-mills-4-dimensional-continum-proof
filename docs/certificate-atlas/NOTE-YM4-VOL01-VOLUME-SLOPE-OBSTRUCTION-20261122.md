# YM4-VOL01 — local magnetic first-variation comparison at spatial sizes L=2,3,4,5

**2026-11-22. Evidence class: exact integer support combinatorics, awaiting hosted replay.** This is the first larger-spatial-volume probe after WG09's fixed-L=2 small-coupling first-neutral Wilson ordering theorem. It is an obstruction and design input, not a volume-uniform or continuum theorem.

## Model identity

Use the original continuous SU(2) Wilson transfer, periodic spatial lattice \((\mathbf Z/L\mathbf Z)^3\), one independent positively oriented link per site/axis, all three spatial plaquette orientations and fundamental trace character basis. For each elementary plaquette \(p\), let \(\phi_p=\chi_{1/2}(U_p)\) and \(W_p=\phi_p/2\). Let \(V=\sum_p W_p\).

For distinct normalized plaquette spin networks \(\phi_p,\phi_q\), any nonzero linkwise Haar integral \(\langle\phi_p,W_r\phi_q\rangle\) requires **even fundamental multiplicity on every link**. Thus its supports must satisfy \(m_p\oplus m_q=m_r\), where \(m\) denotes the set of links with nontrivial fundamental occupation. This is a *necessary* filter, sufficient here to show vanishing.

## Exact geometry and combinatorial results

For **every \(L\ge3\)**, distinct elementary plaquettes share **at most one oriented link**. Hence their symmetric difference occupies 6 or 8 links, whereas any elementary plaquette occupies exactly four. Therefore \(m_p\oplus m_q\ne m_r\). Diagonal \(\langle\phi_p,W_r\phi_p\rangle\) also vanishes because the single plaquette insertion has odd link occupancy. Consequently,

\[
P_{\mathrm{plaq}}VP_{\mathrm{plaq}}=0
\qquad\text{on the fundamental single-plaquette spin-network span for }L\ge3.
\]

The WG06 L=2 positive first magnetic variation is generated by the **additional 18 pairs of two-link winding loops**, creating six \(K_{4,2}\) blocks joining plaquette and double-winding support. When L>=3, an elementary periodic winding has length L, so two disjoint windings have at least 2L>4 fundamental occupied links. The exceptional L=2 rank-42 magnetic graph and its six positive slopes consequently **cannot be carried unchanged** into larger-volume four-fundamental electric bands.

For L>=3, a neutral gauge singlet with exactly four fundamental occupied links and no higher-spin links must form a length-four closed graph cycle. For L>=3, these are elementary contractible plaquettes or (only when L=4) axial length-four winding cycles. The L=3 axial length-three windings are also globally centre-charged and occur at a distinct electric order. This is a graph and sector statement, not yet a spectral ordering theorem.

## Exact second-order cancellation after subtracting the physical vacuum block

There is an additional all-\(L\ge3\) local combinatorial identity. For any two *distinct* plaquettes \(i,j\), the only ordered elementary plaquette pairs \((p,q)\) satisfying
\[
m_p\oplus m_q=m_i\oplus m_j
\]
are \((p,q)=(i,j)\) and \((j,i)\). Indeed a distinct alternative pair would yield four distinct plaquettes whose modulo-two boundaries cancel. Pick one of these four faces. Its four edges would each need to occur in another selected face, but each other face shares at most one link with it. Three other faces cannot cover all four links, a contradiction. This argument is valid for **all** spatial \(L\ge3\), independently of the finite-grid checks at 3,4,5.

The genuine continuous-SU(2) character identity \(\chi_{1/2}(U)^2=1+\chi_1(U)\) and the existence of a link unique to each of two distinct plaquettes yield
\[
\langle\chi_{1/2}(U_i)^2\chi_{1/2}(U_j)^2\rangle=1.
\]
The cross term involving both spin-one characters vanishes by Haar integration on that exclusive link. Therefore each of the two parity-admissible ordered insertions satisfies
\[
\langle\phi_i,W_iW_j\phi_j\rangle=\tfrac14.
\]
The direct second-order off-diagonal contribution is consequently \( \tfrac12(2\cdot\tfrac14)=\tfrac14\). Since the vacuum coupling is \(v_i=\langle\phi_i,V1\rangle=\tfrac12\), the vacuum Schur counterterm is \(v_iv_j=\tfrac14\). Hence the *entire second-order band coefficient* on the distinct single-plaquette states is exactly zero off diagonal,
\[
\boxed{\langle\phi_i,(\tfrac12V^2-VP_{\rm vac}V)\phi_j\rangle=0,\quad i\ne j,\ L\ge3.}
\]
Spatial translations and axis permutations act transitively on single plaquettes, so all diagonal entries of this band coefficient are equal. The complete second-order coefficient is therefore **scalar** on the \(3L^3\)-dimensional single-plaquette band.

For the full physical Schur operator, all other neutral electric sectors have electric spin-index sum at least six (at L>=3) and their normalized eigenvalues are \(O(\beta^2)\) relative to the four-fundamental band as \(\beta\to0\); thus their second-order Feshbach contribution starts at order \(\beta^4\). An explicit uniform-in-\(L\) numerical bound and exact third-order connected matrix are the next obligations.

The independent integer test also counts the parity-admissible two-plaquette insertion pairs for every distinct pair at L=3,4,5, finding exactly two, with an L=2 negative control allowing four. The Haar cancellation follows algebraically as above; it is not inferred from support counts alone.

## Where the next proof must move

At L>=3, the first two magnetic coefficients on the neutral single-plaquette band are **scalar** after the vacuum Schur counterterm, so any symmetry splitting starts **no earlier than third order**. The original physical high-spin resolvent enters at fourth order or later relative to the band. The WG09 six-mode third-order symmetry algebra belongs to the exceptional L=2 winding topology.

The next verifiable mathematical object is the centred third-order local plaquette kernel

\[
H_{3,L}=P_{\rm plaq}\big(\tfrac16V^3-\tfrac12(VP_{\rm vac}V^2+V^2P_{\rm vac}V)\big)P_{\rm plaq},
\]

with each matrix element organized by overlapping plaquette clusters and a **volume-independent bound on the connected remainder**. Direct global norm estimates \(\|V\|\le3L^3\) deteriorate with volume and should be replaced by connected/cumulant or local polymer bounds.
